# Phase 3 · Research Month ⭐ (≈4 weeks)

**This is the differentiator.** Most internships and bootcamps never touch real research. This month gave interns a genuine taste of it — the single biggest reason the program bridges academia and industry.

> ⭐ *This is the part of the program to write up most fully — it's what makes the repo distinctive. Fill in the actual papers/topics interns worked on.*

## The idea
Applied skill without research literacy caps out fast. One focused month teaches interns to **read the frontier, reproduce it, and question it** — habits that make them better engineers *and* open the door to graduate research.

## What interns did
1. **Read like a researcher.** The three-pass method; navigating arXiv, Papers with Code, and Google Scholar; extracting the core contribution of a paper.
2. **Reproduce a result.** Take a paper (or a component of one) and re-implement it — the fastest way to truly understand a method and to learn why reported results are hard to match.
3. **Extend or interrogate it.** A small original tweak, ablation, or honest critique. Interns learned that "it didn't reproduce cleanly" is itself a finding.
4. **Communicate it.** A short technical report structured like a real paper: motivation, method, experiments, honest limitations.

## Why it works as a bridge
- **For the industry-bound:** they leave able to read and implement new papers — a rare, high-value skill on any team.
- **For the research-bound:** they get a low-stakes first taste of the research loop before committing to a thesis or PhD.

## Deliverable
A reproduction-plus-extension write-up on a chosen paper, with runnable code, posted publicly.

## Assessed on
Depth of understanding, rigor of the reproduction, and honesty about what did and didn't work. See the [rubric](../evaluation/rubric.md).
