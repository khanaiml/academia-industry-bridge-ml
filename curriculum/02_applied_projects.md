# Phase 2 · Applied Projects (≈8 weeks)

**Goal:** move from exercises to real end-to-end projects — owning everything from raw data to a working model, the way industry actually works.

> ⚙️ *Swap in the real project domains your interns worked on (e.g. lead scoring, computer vision, LLM automation) — these tie the repo to actual TABSAP work.*

## What interns built
Interns took a problem from messy reality to a working solution, owning every stage:
- **Data engineering:** sourcing, cleaning, validation, feature building.
- **Modeling:** choosing an approach, iterating, tuning (e.g. with Optuna).
- **Evaluation against a goal:** not "what's my accuracy" but "does this solve the problem."
- **Domain exposure** across the kinds of applied problems the team handled — e.g. predictive/tabular modeling, computer vision pipelines, and LLM-powered automation.

## Emphasis
- Deep learning where it earns its place (PyTorch/TensorFlow), classical methods where they win.
- Reproducibility and experiment tracking (e.g. MLflow) so results aren't lost.
- Working in a shared codebase with real git hygiene.

## Deliverable
One substantial applied project per intern, end to end, with tracked experiments and a written report framing the problem, approach, results, and limitations.

## Assessed on
Ownership across the full stack, soundness of the modeling decisions, and quality of the written report. See the [rubric](../evaluation/rubric.md).
